# LuminaNexus Hebrew Tracks

## Program Phrase

**Hebrew for Every Grade, Every Beginning.**

## Track Philosophy

LuminaNexus uses flexible Hebrew learning tracks so students can begin at the right doorway, regardless of grade level or previous experience.

Students are placed by Hebrew readiness, not shame, age, or embarrassment.

A high school student who has never studied Hebrew may begin in Aleph Track with age-appropriate pacing and examples. A younger student who already recognizes letters may begin in Bet Track. A student who can already decode simple Hebrew words may begin in Gimel Track.

The goal is simple:

**Every student begins somewhere, and every beginning deserves dignity.**

## Aleph Track — First Beginning

### Who It Is For

Aleph Track is for total beginners at any grade level.

This includes students who:

* Have never seen Hebrew before
* Do not yet know that Hebrew reads from right to left
* Cannot yet recognize Hebrew letters
* May know that Hebrew exists but cannot read or write it
* Need confidence before moving into vowels or syllables

### Main Focus

Aleph Track focuses on:

* Hebrew directionality
* Letter recognition
* Letter names
* Basic sounds
* Visual familiarity
* Names in Hebrew
* Simple Hebrew words
* Confidence and comfort

### Sample Learning Goals

By the end of Aleph Track, students should begin to:

* Recognize that Hebrew is read from right to left
* Identify several major Hebrew letters
* Recognize Aleph, Bet, Mem, Shin, Lamed, and Tav
* Understand that Hebrew letters have names and sounds
* Recognize simple Hebrew words by sight
* See how Hebrew letters can form names
* Feel that Hebrew is approachable and not frightening

### Age-Appropriate Use

Aleph Track should never feel childish for older students.

For younger students, Aleph Track may use movement, songs, coloring, tracing, and letter games.

For older students, Aleph Track may move faster and include cultural context, word patterns, roots, names, and meaning.

The track is beginner-level, but the dignity of the student always comes first.

## Bet Track — Building the Word

### Who It Is For

Bet Track is for students who know some Hebrew letters and are ready to begin building words.

This includes students who:

* Recognize several Hebrew letters
* Understand that Hebrew reads right to left
* May know some letter sounds
* Are ready to learn basic vowels
* Are ready to combine letters and sounds into syllables
* Need practice decoding simple Hebrew words

### Main Focus

Bet Track focuses on:

* Review of Hebrew letters
* Basic niqqud/vowels
* Syllables
* Decoding
* Simple vocabulary
* Word families
* Names and familiar words
* Early root awareness

### Sample Learning Goals

By the end of Bet Track, students should begin to:

* Recognize most Hebrew letters
* Identify several basic vowel marks
* Combine consonants and vowels into syllables
* Read simple syllables aloud
* Decode short Hebrew words
* Recognize common vocabulary
* Understand that many Hebrew words grow from roots
* Begin reading with patience and confidence

### Age-Appropriate Use

For younger students, Bet Track may use sound games, matching cards, songs, and simple writing practice.

For older students, Bet Track may include faster decoding drills, name studies, root patterns, transliteration comparison, and simple cultural vocabulary.

Bet Track is where Hebrew begins to move from symbol recognition into actual reading.

## Gimel Track — Reading with Meaning

### Who It Is For

Gimel Track is for students who can recognize Hebrew letters and are ready to read words, phrases, and short passages with meaning.

This includes students who:

* Know most Hebrew letters
* Have some familiarity with vowels
* Can decode simple syllables
* Are ready for short words and phrases
* Want to understand Hebrew roots and meaning
* Are ready for cultural and interpretive context

### Main Focus

Gimel Track focuses on:

* Reading fluency
* Vocabulary expansion
* Hebrew roots
* Word families
* Short phrases
* Cultural context
* Meaning and interpretation
* Confidence reading aloud

### Sample Learning Goals

By the end of Gimel Track, students should begin to:

* Read short Hebrew words with increasing confidence
* Recognize common roots
* Understand how roots can shape word meaning
* Read simple Hebrew phrases
* Connect vocabulary to culture and story
* Use Hebrew words in simple classroom activities
* Understand that Hebrew is a language of structure, memory, and meaning

### Age-Appropriate Use

For younger students, Gimel Track may use simple phrases, familiar vocabulary, songs, and classroom objects.

For older students, Gimel Track may include roots, short cultural texts, names, historical context, and guided interpretation.

Gimel Track helps students move from reading letters to discovering meaning.

## Track Movement

Students may move between tracks as they grow.

The tracks are not walls. They are doorways.

A student may begin in Aleph Track, review through Bet Track, and touch Gimel Track concepts within the same school year, depending on readiness.

A mixed-age classroom may contain students from all three tracks. In that case, lessons should include:

* A shared opening
* Track-based practice
* A whole-group activity
* A confidence-building review
* A simple take-home step

## Placement Questions

ChavrutaGPT, teachers, or program guides may help identify a student’s starting point by asking:

1. Have you seen Hebrew letters before?
2. Do you know that Hebrew reads from right to left?
3. Can you recognize Aleph?
4. Can you recognize Bet?
5. Can you recognize Mem or Shin?
6. Do you know any Hebrew vowel marks?
7. Can you read a simple syllable like בָּ or מָ?
8. Can you read any Hebrew words?
9. Are you learning for school, family, culture, faith, curiosity, or enrichment?
10. Would you rather start slowly, move quickly, or review first?

## Placement Guidance

If a student knows no Hebrew letters, begin with Aleph Track.

If a student knows several letters but cannot yet read syllables, begin with Bet Track.

If a student can recognize letters and read some simple syllables or words, begin with Gimel Track.

If uncertain, begin gently with Aleph review and move forward quickly as the student shows readiness.

## Key Message

No student is late to Hebrew.

Every letter is a beginning.

Every beginning deserves a clear next step.
