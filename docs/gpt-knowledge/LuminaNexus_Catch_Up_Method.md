# LuminaNexus Catch-Up Method

## Program Phrase

**Hebrew for Every Grade, Every Beginning.**

## Core Idea

LuminaNexus is designed so students can enter Hebrew learning at any age and quickly find their place.

A student is not “behind” because they are beginning.

Beginning is part of the design.

The LuminaNexus catch-up method places students by readiness, not shame, age, or grade level.

## Guiding Sentence

**No student is late to Hebrew. Every letter is a beginning.**

## Why Catch-Up Matters

Many students may enter the program after others have already begun.

Some may join in a later grade.

Some may miss earlier lessons.

Some may know a few Hebrew letters from home, synagogue, family, music, culture, or curiosity.

Some may know nothing at all.

The program must be able to welcome each student without making them feel embarrassed.

The catch-up method gives every student a doorway.

## Placement by Readiness

Students should be placed according to what they can currently recognize and do.

Placement should consider:

* Letter recognition
* Directionality
* Sound awareness
* Vowel familiarity
* Syllable reading
* Word decoding
* Confidence
* Age and maturity
* Learning pace
* Reason for learning

Grade level matters for tone and age-appropriate examples, but it should not be the main placement tool.

## The Three Catch-Up Doorways

### Aleph Doorway

Use the Aleph Doorway when a student:

* Has never studied Hebrew
* Does not recognize Hebrew letters
* Does not know Hebrew reads from right to left
* Needs visual familiarity and confidence
* Feels nervous or unsure

Aleph Doorway focus:

* Directionality
* Letter shapes
* Letter names
* Basic sounds
* Names in Hebrew
* Simple words
* Confidence

### Bet Doorway

Use the Bet Doorway when a student:

* Recognizes some Hebrew letters
* Knows Hebrew reads from right to left
* May know some sounds
* Does not yet read syllables confidently
* Is ready for vowels and decoding

Bet Doorway focus:

* Letter review
* Basic vowels
* Syllables
* Sound blending
* Short words
* Word families
* Early root awareness

### Gimel Doorway

Use the Gimel Doorway when a student:

* Recognizes most Hebrew letters
* Knows some vowels
* Can read simple syllables
* Can decode short words
* Is ready for short phrases and meaning

Gimel Doorway focus:

* Reading fluency
* Vocabulary
* Roots
* Short phrases
* Cultural context
* Meaning and interpretation

## Quick Placement Questions

Teachers or ChavrutaGPT may ask:

1. Have you seen Hebrew letters before?
2. Do you know which direction Hebrew reads?
3. Can you recognize Aleph?
4. Can you recognize Bet?
5. Can you recognize Mem?
6. Can you recognize Shin?
7. Do you know any Hebrew vowel marks?
8. Can you read a simple syllable?
9. Can you read any Hebrew words?
10. Do you want to start slowly, review quickly, or try a challenge?

These questions should feel friendly, not like a test.

## Quick Placement Guidance

If a student knows no Hebrew letters, begin with Aleph.

If a student knows several letters but cannot read syllables, begin with Bet.

If a student knows letters and can read simple syllables or words, begin with Gimel.

If unsure, begin with Aleph review and move forward quickly as readiness appears.

## Catch-Up Session Model

A short catch-up session may take 15 to 20 minutes.

### Step 1: Welcome

Tell the student:

“You are not behind. This is your beginning.”

### Step 2: Check Directionality

Show a Hebrew word and ask which way Hebrew reads.

Teach or review right-to-left reading.

### Step 3: Check Letter Recognition

Show a few common letters:

* א Aleph
* ב Bet
* מ Mem
* ש Shin
* ל Lamed
* ת Tav

Ask which ones the student recognizes.

### Step 4: Check Sound Readiness

Ask whether the student knows any sounds.

Do not pressure them.

### Step 5: Give One Success

Choose one small task the student can complete.

Examples:

* Recognize Aleph
* Trace Bet
* Find Mem in a word
* Read one syllable
* Build a name
* Match a Hebrew word to a meaning

### Step 6: Assign a Track

Gently assign a starting point:

* Aleph Track
* Bet Track
* Gimel Track

Explain that tracks are doorways, not labels.

## Fast Catch-Up for Older Students

Older beginners should not be given childish materials unless they enjoy them.

For middle and high school students:

* Use mature examples
* Move faster when possible
* Explain patterns
* Use names and roots
* Include cultural and historical context
* Respect their intelligence
* Avoid babyish language
* Avoid embarrassment

An older beginner may start with Aleph, but the tone should honor their age.

## Fast Catch-Up for Younger Students

Younger students may need more repetition, movement, and visual practice.

Use:

* Tracing
* Coloring
* Matching
* Singing
* Movement
* Letter cards
* Sound games
* Simple praise

The goal is comfort and confidence.

## Catch-Up Within a Mixed Classroom

When a new student enters an existing group:

1. Welcome the student publicly but assess privately when possible.
2. Give the student a simple role in the group activity.
3. Provide a short Aleph/Bet/Gimel placement check.
4. Give a catch-up card or worksheet.
5. Pair the student with a kind peer when appropriate.
6. Celebrate one small success.

Never announce that the student is behind.

## Catch-Up Activities

Possible catch-up activities include:

* Hebrew directionality card
* Aleph recognition sheet
* Name-in-Hebrew activity
* Letter matching game
* Vowel sound mini-chart
* Syllable ladder
* Root discovery card
* Short vocabulary review
* Read-one-word challenge
* Personal progress card

## ChavrutaGPT Catch-Up Behavior

When ChavrutaGPT helps a student catch up, it should:

1. Reassure the student
2. Ask a few gentle placement questions
3. Identify a likely track
4. Give one small lesson
5. Offer one practice step
6. Encourage the student to continue

ChavrutaGPT should not overload the student.

ChavrutaGPT should not make the student feel late, slow, or behind.

## Recommended ChavrutaGPT Opening

“Wonderful. Let’s find your Hebrew doorway. You are not behind. I just need to see where your beginning is.”

## Key Message

Catch-up is not repair.

Catch-up is welcome.

Every student begins somewhere, and LuminaNexus is built to meet them there.
