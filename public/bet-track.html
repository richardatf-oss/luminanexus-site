Ready for Bet

Start here when you can find Hebrew's starting side, recognize several letters, and use the ahhh Stick Vowels and eee Pea Vowel with help. Each lesson introduces any new consonants before using them. This course develops decoding; it does not repeat Aleph's tracing sequence.

If a foundation feels unfamiliar, review it in Aleph and return. Read the letters and vowel marks before looking to the picture for meaning.

The online lessons and printable workbook follow the same 32 lesson sequence. Sound labels use our class cues. Community pronunciation differences are respected.

Bet Lesson 1 · Workbook pages 2 and 3

Blend ahhh and eee

Goal Blend a familiar consonant and vowel without guessing a whole word.

Meet the sounds you will use

מ

Mem says m.

א

Aleph carries the vowel sound.

בּ ב

Bet says b; Vet says v.

Learn before you practice

The stick and the stick with a handle say ahhh in these examples. The pea beneath says eee.

Find the letter, look beneath, then join the sounds: m plus ahhh makes mah; m plus eee makes mee.

A dot inside Mem keeps its m sound. A final Aleph is quiet in our two family words.

Watch the word grow

מַ מִ

mah / mee

בַּ בִּ בִ

bah / bee / vee

אִמָּא אַבָּא

ee-ma / ah-ba

Read and show meaning

אִמָּאmother
אַבָּאfather
  1. Read each small sound before blending the whole word.
  2. Point to the mother and father in the picture. Read their labels.
  3. Say each word again without using the picture to guess.

Try it yourself

Which syllable says mee?

Which word means father?

Readiness check I can blend ahhh and eee and read two family words.

Bet Lesson 2 · Workbook pages 4 and 5

Close a word with a consonant

Goal Read the final consonant without adding an extra vowel.

Meet the sounds you will use

י

Yud says y at the start of these words.

ד

Dalet says d.

מ ם

Mem changes to ם at the end; it still says m.

Learn before you practice

Read a letter and its vowel, then close with the last consonant. Do not add ahhh after the final letter.

Yud with the ahhh handle, followed by final Mem, makes yam: sea. Yud plus ahhh and Dalet makes yad: hand.

Yud can also help write the eee sound after another letter. In מִי, the whole word says mee, meaning who.

Watch the word grow

יָ ם

yah + m = yam

יָ ד

yah + d = yad

מִי

mee = who

Read and show meaning

יָםsea
יָדhand
מִיwho
  1. Touch the rightmost letter first.
  2. Blend the vowel, then finish with m or d.
  3. Use the sea and hand picture to check meaning after reading.

Try it yourself

Which word means sea?

How does יָד end?

Readiness check I can close yam and yad without adding a vowel.

Bet Lesson 4 · Workbook pages 8 and 9

Read the belly vowel oo

Goal Read both oo patterns and decode two pictured words.

Meet the sounds you will use

ס

Samech says s.

ת

Tav says t. A dot inside does not change its t sound.

וּ

Vav with a dot in its belly says oo.

Learn before you practice

A dot touching Vav at its belly gives oo, as in moon. Say oo without adding v.

Three descending dots beneath a letter also give oo. The two Mem models below both say moo.

Keep oo smooth between the first and last consonants: s-oo-s and t-oo-t.

Bet Lesson 5 · Workbook pages 10 and 11

Read eh and ay carefully

Goal Distinguish the three eggs from the two side-by-side dots.

Meet the sounds you will use

ל

Lamed says l.

ח

Chet says kh, a throat sound as in Bach.

נ ן

Nun says n; final ן keeps the n sound.

Learn before you practice

Three eggs beneath the letter give eh, as in eggs. The two side-by-side dots use our class cue ay, as in brake.

Many modern Hebrew speakers pronounce both signs as eh. Follow one teacher model consistently; do not mark a familiar community pronunciation wrong.

Use final Mem and final Nun to close the words. Pictures check meaning; the marks tell you how to read.

Watch the word grow

לֶ

leh

לֶ חֶ ם

leh + kheh + m

לֶחֶם בֵּן

leh-khem = bread / bayn = son

Read and show meaning

לֶחֶםbread
בֵּןson
  1. Point to each vowel and say eh or the class ay cue.
  2. Read bread slowly, joining the last m without adding a vowel.
  3. Read son with your teacher: class bayn; often ben in modern Hebrew.

Try it yourself

Which word means bread?

Which mark has three eggs?

Readiness check I can distinguish the marks and read bread and son with my teacher.

Bet Lesson 6 · Workbook pages 12 and 13

Read a greeting with meaning

Goal Blend known patterns into shalom and two short greetings.

Meet the sounds you will use

שׁ

Shin has its dot on the right and says sh.

ם

Final Mem closes with m, without an extra vowel.

Learn before you practice

Find the right-hand dot on Shin. This dot gives sh; the mark beneath supplies the vowel.

Blend sh plus ahhh, then l plus oh, then close with m: sha-lom.

Shalom can mean peace and is also used as hello or goodbye. Use the greeting scene to act out hello.

Watch the word grow

שָׁלוֹם

sha-lom = peace / hello / goodbye

שָׁלוֹם אַבָּא

sha-lom ah-ba = hello, Dad

Read and show meaning

שָׁלוֹםpeace / hello / goodbye
  1. Read shalom without a picture hint.
  2. Read each greeting, then tell whom you greeted.
  3. Act out one greeting with a study partner.
