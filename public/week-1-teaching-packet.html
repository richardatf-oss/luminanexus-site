Week 1 Teaching Packet

A flexible first session for a mixed-readiness group. Everyone practices careful looking, listening, and respectful Hebrew reading; students work at the doorway that fits them today.

Keep the room together; differentiate the task.

Use one shared theme and let each student show learning at a comfortable level. A track is a starting point, not a label or a race.

One-hour lesson rhythm

0–5 minutes · Welcome
Invite each learner to share a language, word, or name they enjoy. Introduce the idea that Hebrew has its own letters and reading direction.
5–12 minutes · Notice
Show a short Hebrew line. Model starting at the right and moving left. Students point, trace in the air, or explain what they notice.
12–20 minutes · Model
Teach the common routine: look at the letters and marks, listen to a trusted model, try together, then explain what you noticed.
20–38 minutes · Track work
Use the track selector below to focus the activity cards. In a mixed group, students may work in parallel while the teacher checks in briefly with each doorway.
38–50 minutes · Partner practice
Partners take turns being the reader and the careful observer. The observer asks, “What did you notice in the letters?”
50–57 minutes · Share
Each learner shares one thing they can recognize or one question they want to carry forward.
57–60 minutes · Close
Celebrate effort. Record an observation for next time; do not rank students or turn the quick-check into a grade.

Aleph · First Beginning

Find the Hebrew starting place

Goal: Follow Hebrew from the right edge toward the left and recognize a taught letter.

א　ב　ג

Point to the rightmost letter first. Name any letter you already know; it is fine to point without naming.

  1. Teacher models the direction once.
  2. Learner traces the path with a finger or eyes.
  3. Learner identifies one familiar shape or describes its features.
Teacher cue and answer

Reading begins at the right. The first letter in this display is Aleph, א. Accept pointing, naming, or careful shape description as evidence of participation.

Bet · Building Words

Blend a familiar sound pattern

Goal: Read a short, fully pointed word from right to left using letters and vowel cues already taught.

בַּת

Look at the dot in Bet and the vowel beneath it. Read the letters before checking the meaning.

  1. Point to Bet on the right and follow each mark.
  2. Blend the modeled sounds slowly, then smoothly.
  3. Tell a partner which visual cue helped.
Teacher cue and answer

בַּת is bat, “daughter.” The dot in Bet marks the b sound in this pointed example; patach beneath it cues ah. Use the class pronunciation model and distinguish a memory cue from a formal rule.

Gimel · Reading with Meaning

See, read, mean

Goal: Decode a short known word and explain its meaning without relying on a picture guess.

דָּג

First read the word. Then say what it means and name one letter or vowel mark that helped.

  1. Read from the right, noticing each letter and mark.
  2. Say the word aloud or silently decode it.
  3. Explain the meaning and point to the evidence in the word.
Teacher cue and answer

דָּג is dag, “fish.” The learner should connect the decoded word to its meaning and identify a textual clue. A picture may confirm meaning afterward, but should not replace decoding.

Teacher language

Invite effort

“Take your time. Show me what you notice first.”

Support a retry

“Let’s look at the letters and marks together, then try it once more.”

Offer a challenge

“Can you explain which letter or vowel cue helped you read it?”

Continue the lesson
