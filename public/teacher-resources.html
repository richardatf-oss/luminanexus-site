    Put the program to work

    A practical first four weeks.

    Use this as a planning framework, rather than a deadline for a learner to master a skill. Mixed-readiness groups can work on different track lessons within the same session.

    1. Before the first session: read the Teacher Guide, prepare a few letter cards, and print the placement quick-check. Decide how you will model pronunciation consistently.
    2. Week 1: use the teaching packet to welcome learners and observe what feels familiar. Choose starting tracks without ranking students.
    3. Week 2: pick one goal from each learner’s track. Model before independent practice, and introduce a vowel before asking learners to use it.
    4. Week 3: repeat the skill with a new example drawn from the same lesson. Ask learners to explain what helped them read.
    5. Week 4: revisit the placement guidance and choose whether to repeat, continue, or change tracks. Record a specific observation rather than a general label.
    Read the assessment guidance

    A few calm minutes at home

    Practice without turning home into a test.

    Look, model, try, notice.

    Open the same lesson the learner used in class. Ask them to find one familiar letter or vowel. Model its sound, try one word together, then let the learner show one thing independently.

    Stop while the experience is still comfortable. Repeating one useful example is more valuable than rushing through unfamiliar marks.

    Send a useful question back.

    Instead of “They cannot read,” try “They found the starting side independently and needed a model for this vowel.” A specific observation gives the teacher a place to begin next time.

    Use the workbook for pencil practice and the online lesson for checks. Chavruta can help explain the lesson when an adult reviews the response.
