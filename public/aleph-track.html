My Hebrew Beginning

Hebrew is new to my eyes. That is okay. Every student begins somewhere. Every letter is a beginning.

In this workbook, I will learn to:

  • see Hebrew letters
  • follow Hebrew from right to left
  • trace letters carefully
  • find letters inside words
  • notice small marks
  • ask good questions
  • grow with courage

I do not have to know everything today. I begin with one step. One letter. One doorway. One little light.

Lesson 1

Opening the Hebrew Doorway

Hebrew Begins on the Right

Big Idea

Hebrew begins on the right side of the page.

Look at the arrow.

←

Hebrew moves this way.

Teacher says:

“When we read Hebrew, our eyes begin on the right and move toward the left.”

Student says:

“Hebrew begins on the right.”

Point and Say

Point to the right side of the page.

Say: “This is where Hebrew begins.”

Now move your finger slowly across the page from right to left.

Practice the Hebrew Direction

Use your finger first. Then use your pencil.

Start here:

RIGHT SIDE ← ← ← ← LEFT SIDE

Trace the arrows:

← ← ← ← ←

Circle the Starting Side

Circle the side where Hebrew begins.

LEFT SIDE     RIGHT SIDE

Teacher Question

Where do your Hebrew eyes begin?

Student Answer

My Hebrew eyes begin on the side.

Confidence Check

Color one light.

  • ○ I am still learning.
  • ○ I can point to the right side.
  • ○ I can show the Hebrew direction.

Lesson 2

Hebrew Letters Are Not English Letters

Big Idea

Hebrew letters do not look like English letters. That is okay. English has its own letter shapes. Hebrew has its own letter shapes too.

We do not need Hebrew letters to look like A, B, or C. We only need to look carefully and learn them one by one.

Teacher says:

“Hebrew is its own language. Its letters have their own beautiful shapes.”

Student says:

“Hebrew letters are different, and I can learn them.”

Look and Notice

English letters look one way.

A B C

Hebrew letters look another way.

א ב ע

Both are real letters. Both are good to learn. They are not the same.

Careful Looking

Look at these Hebrew letters:

א ב ג ע

They may be new to your eyes. That is okay.

Circle the Hebrew Letters

Circle the letters that are Hebrew:

A ג B א C ד ב

Teacher Question

Do Hebrew letters look exactly like English letters?

Student Answer

Yes / No

I Can Say

“Hebrew letters have their own shapes.”

Confidence Check

  • ○ I am still noticing.
  • ○ I can find Hebrew letters.
  • ○ I can tell Hebrew and English apart.

Chavruta Question

Ask Chavruta or your teacher:

“How are Hebrew letters different from English letters?”

Closing Sentence

Today I learned that Hebrew letters have their own shapes.

Lesson 3

Aleph and Ayin

The Quiet Letters

Big Idea

Aleph and Ayin are Hebrew letters. These letters are special. They are often quiet letters. That means they do not usually make a strong sound by themselves. They help carry the vowel sound.

Aleph is:

א

Ayin is:

ע

Teacher says:

“Aleph and Ayin are quiet letters. Quiet does not mean unimportant.”

Student says:

“Aleph and Ayin are quiet letters.”

Look and Notice

Look at Aleph. Look at Ayin. They are not the same.

Aleph looks one way. Ayin looks another way.

Circle the Aleph

Circle every Aleph.

א ע א ב א

Circle the Ayin

Circle every Ayin.

ע א ע ב ע

Trace Aleph

Trace the Aleph letters.

א א א

Trace Ayin

Trace the Ayin letters.

ע ע ע

Same or Different?

Put a check mark if they are the same.

  • א א ______
  • ע ע ______
  • א ע ______
  • ע א ______

Teacher Question

Do Aleph and Ayin look the same?

Student Answer

Yes / No

Chavruta Question

Ask Chavruta or your teacher:

“What is the difference between Aleph and Ayin?”

Closing Sentence

Today I learned Aleph and Ayin.

Lesson 4

Bet and Vet

The Dot Helps Us See

Big Idea

Bet and Vet look almost the same. Bet has a dot. Vet has no dot. The dot helps us know which letter we are seeing.

Bet is:

בּ

Vet is:

ב

Teacher says:

“Bet and Vet have the same shape. The dot helps us tell them apart.”

Student says:

“The dot helps me see Bet.”

Look and Notice

Look at Bet. Look at Vet. They have the same letter shape. Bet has a dot inside. Vet has an empty space inside.

Bet has the ball. Vet has the void.

Point and Say

Point to Bet. Say: “Bet.”

Point to the dot. Say: “Dot.”

Point to Vet. Say: “Vet.”

Now say: “Bet has a dot. Vet does not.”

Circle the Bet

Circle every Bet.

בּ ב בּ א בּ

Circle the Vet

Circle every Vet.

ב בּ ע ב ב

Teacher Question

How can you tell Bet and Vet apart?

Student Answer

Bet has a .

Vet has no .

I Can Say

“Bet has a dot. Vet has no dot.”

Confidence Check

  • ○ I am still learning.
  • ○ I can find the dot.
  • ○ I can tell Bet and Vet apart.

Chavruta Question

Ask Chavruta or your teacher:

“How are Bet and Vet different?”

Closing Sentence

Today I learned that Bet has a dot and Vet has no dot.
