Our mission

No student is late to Hebrew.

LuminaNexus Foundation provides accessible Hebrew education for K–12 students through Ivrit HaOr, Hebrew of Light. Our weekly enrichment model helps learners enter at any grade level and find a starting point based on readiness.

Some students begin with the direction of a line and the shape of a letter. Others are ready to blend sounds or read a phrase. Aleph, Bet, and Gimel offer three beginnings, with room to repeat, practice, and grow.

Names, useful words, cultural context, and thoughtful questions give learners something meaningful to connect with. Language facts, traditional interpretations, and creative reflections are kept distinct.

Ivrit HaOr in the classroom

A practical rhythm for real classrooms.

A weekly hour of learning

Welcome, review, introduce one skill, practice together, and close with a small reflection. Teachers can adapt this rhythm for schools, homeschool groups, enrichment programs, and mixed-readiness classrooms.

Online lessons and printable workbooks follow matching sequences. Each new vowel is introduced before students use it independently.

Prepare with confidence

The Teacher Guide explains the lesson rhythm, classroom approach, track maps, and assessment. The placement quick-check helps you observe readiness without turning a starting point into a label.

Pilot program

A classroom beginning in Kingman, Arizona.

Our pilot phase brings Ivrit HaOr to Desert Ridge Preparatory in Kingman, Arizona. It explores how the Aleph, Bet, and Gimel tracks can support students of different ages and levels of Hebrew familiarity in a flexible classroom setting.

We are grateful to Desert Ridge Preparatory for welcoming a program built around approachable learning, steady practice, and growing confidence.

Our approach to Jewish study

Curiosity, context, and care.

Torah belongs to Israel. We approach Jewish sources with gratitude and respect for Jewish tradition and rabbinic authority. The Source Library, 231 Gates explorer, and optional Noahide daily study path help visitors read, ask questions, and distinguish a source from their own reflection.
