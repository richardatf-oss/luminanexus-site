How to use this library

One source, one careful question

These cards support literacy, cultural learning, and teacher preparation. They are not religious rulings. You can read Hebrew, an available translation, or both; you do not need to finish every prompt.

  1. Choose a card that fits your readiness.
  2. Open its source and read a short passage.
  3. Use the prompt, then mark the card explored.
  4. Return to your track when you want more practice.

Hebrew words

Shalom · שָׁלוֹם

Explore a familiar Hebrew word through its spelling, sound, and different uses.

BeginningBuilding
Open guided study

Learning context: שָׁלוֹם is used in greetings and can also refer to peace or well-being, depending on context.

Vocabulary: Identify Shin, Lamed, Vav, and Mem. Notice that this word ends with the regular Mem form.

Try this: Say the word after a trusted pronunciation model. Find one place where it is used as a greeting and another where a translation conveys peace or well-being.

Ask: How does the surrounding sentence help you choose the meaning?

Check the word in a reliable Hebrew dictionary or lexicon; do not infer meaning from letter shapes alone.

Letters + belonging

Names in Hebrew

Use a learner’s name as a welcoming bridge to letter forms and right-to-left direction.

BeginningTeacher prep
Open guided study

Teaching context: Hebrew spelling of names can vary by pronunciation and transliteration. Check the preferred spelling with the learner or family; avoid presenting one spelling as the only possible form.

Try this: Point to each letter from right to left. Let the learner match a familiar letter, trace the name, or describe one shape.

Ask: Which letter do you recognize? What would you like to learn about the others?

Readiness: start with recognition; do not require a learner to disclose or write a personal name.

Torah + Jewish context

The Priestly Blessing

Read a biblical blessing in context and notice how its phrases are arranged.

Confident readerTeacher prep
Open guided study

Source context: Numbers 6:24–26 contains the priestly blessing, a text with a distinct place in Jewish tradition and practice.

Vocabulary: In translation, notice the repeated blessing language and the movement from blessing and guarding to graciousness and peace.

Try this: Read the verses in context. Mark repeated words or phrases and compare them with the Hebrew text if appropriate.

Ask: What pattern do you notice in the words? What belongs to the text, and what belongs to your response?

Cultural boundary: use this for textual learning and context; do not turn it into a new Noahide liturgical requirement.

Torah + Jewish context

Listening · The Shema

Study a short, culturally significant verse with care for its place in Jewish life.

Confident readerTeacher prep
Open guided study

Source context: Deuteronomy 6:4 begins the Shema, a central Jewish declaration and prayer. It should be introduced as Jewish scripture and practice, not relabeled as a Noahide prayer.

Vocabulary: שְׁמַע (Shema) is commonly rendered “hear” or “listen.” Notice how a translation handles the full phrase.

Source-based letter study

The 231 Gates

Explore letter pairs described in Sefer Yetzirah without assigning invented meanings to individual pairs.

Confident readerTeacher prep
Open guided study

Source context: Sefer Yetzirah 2:4 describes the twenty-two foundation letters and their combinations. The explorer presents distinct two-letter pairs for observation.

Try this: Choose one pair. Identify each letter separately, then find a real Hebrew word containing both letters and verify it in a reliable text or lexicon.

Ask: Which observations come from the text, and which are your own? Do not treat resemblance as proof of a word relationship.

This is a doorway into a Jewish mystical text, not a religious ruling or a claim that every pair has a separate traditional interpretation.
